Privacy policy

QuickRemote sends us no data.

This page covers the phone app, the Wear OS watch app, QuickRemote PC and this website. Last updated: 22 October 2026.

In short

  • There is no account, no ads, no statistics and no crash reporting. The apps send nothing to the developer.
  • The phone and the computer talk only on your own local network, over a TLS-encrypted, PIN-protected connection. In Bluetooth mode the phone acts as a keyboard and mouse.
  • The watch app never talks to the computer; everything goes through the phone app.
  • The error log stays on your device. Only you can share it, if you choose to.

Who is responsible

QuickRemote is a personal project by Berat Kaan Akcan. Because the apps send no data to the developer’s servers, the developer receives and keeps none of the personal data that arises while you use them. So there is nothing on our side for you to access, correct or delete; your data is on your own devices, and you control it.

What is stored on your devices

All of the following stays on the device in question and goes away when you uninstall the app.

Phone app

  • The last five computers you connected to: name, address and PIN. After a connection is approved, the computer’s certificate fingerprint is stored too, to check that it is the same computer next time.
  • Your settings: language, theme, vibrations, talk length, the Bluetooth program you picked.
  • Presentation history: the length of each talk and the time on each slide. It does not store slide content or notes. You can delete entries one by one or all at once in Settings.
  • The error log (see below).

QuickRemote PC

  • The self-signed certificate made for this computer, and your settings (theme, language, tray, start at login, update check, laser setting).
  • The list of connected phones, in memory for that session only; the session PIN is replaced at every start.
  • When a slide picture is taken from the presentation program, it is written briefly to the app’s data folder and deleted once read. Pictures sent to the phone are also cached in memory for a while (at most 30 MB) and are gone when the program closes.
  • The error log (see below).

Watch app

  • The theme you picked and the last state from the phone (connection, slide number, the computer’s name, the clock). It stores nothing else.

The error log

The phone and PC apps write errors and connection events to a log file on the device (at most 1 MB each, at most 3 files; older ones are deleted). Before a line is written, PINs, quickremote:// links, certificate fingerprints and security codes, notes and media titles, and file paths are removed. IP addresses, ports and device names remain. The log is never sent anywhere by itself. You export it yourself with “Share error log” (phone) or “Export log” (PC) in Settings; “Report a problem” opens a pre-filled page on GitHub, and you attach the log there yourself.

What moves between your devices

Wi-Fi mode: phone ⇄ computer

The phone finds the computer on the local network with mDNS, or you scan a QR code or type the address. The connection is encrypted with TLS; QuickRemote PC refuses connections from outside the local network before encryption even starts, and does not let web browsers connect. The phone sends the computer: the PIN, the device name (the name you gave it, or the phone model), commands (next slide, laser movement, volume and so on) and whether it wants slide pictures. The computer sends the phone: the slide number and count, the name of the program running, speaker notes, the slide’s picture and the next one (or a screen picture of a full-screen show), the title and artist of the media now playing, the volume and the computer’s name. You can turn slide pictures off in Settings. None of this goes to the internet.

Bluetooth mode

The phone acts as a keyboard and mouse for the computer through Android’s Bluetooth HID. Only key presses and mouse movement go; the computer sends nothing back. The phone does not scan; you pair it from the computer. The phone’s Bluetooth name is visible to the computer while pairing.

Watch ⇄ phone

The watch never talks to the computer. It sends its commands (next, previous, laser and so on) and the laser movement to the phone, which passes them on over its own connection (Wi-Fi or Bluetooth). The phone sends the watch the state: connection, slide number and count, the computer’s name, the theme and the clock. This travels between the paired watch and phone over Wear OS’s Data Layer (Google Play services) and a Bluetooth socket between them. The watch app asks for no internet permission.

What reaches the internet

The phone and watch apps send nothing to the internet by themselves. QuickRemote PC reaches out only in these cases:

  • Update check. When on (the default), it asks GitHub for the latest release at start and every 24 hours. GitHub sees your IP address then; nothing else that identifies you is sent and nothing is downloaded. You can turn it off in Settings; some builds of QuickRemote PC are made without this check.
  • Links that open in the browser. “Report a problem”, “New version: Download” and similar open your default browser on GitHub when you click them. What follows is governed by GitHub’s privacy policy.
  • WPS setup on Linux. If you press “Install” for WPS support in the setup panel, the Python package pywpsrpc is downloaded from PyPI (a fixed version, checked against hashes).
  • Album art (Linux). If the media player gives a web address for its cover, the computer downloads that picture and passes it to the phone.

This website

The site uses no cookies, loads no trackers or ads, ships its own fonts and makes no requests to a third-party server. It stores only the light/dark choice you make (qr-theme) in your browser. Like any web server, the service that hosts the site may keep ordinary access logs (IP address, time, page requested); we do not see or use them.

Third-party components

  • Google ML Kit (QR scanning). The phone reads the pairing QR code on the device with ML Kit. The image does not leave the device and is not saved. As described in Google’s data disclosure, ML Kit may send technical performance and usage metrics to Google; that is done by the component, not by QuickRemote.
  • Google Play services and Google Play. The Data Layer between watch and phone runs on Google Play services. If you install the app from Google Play, Google handles the install-related data under its own policy.
  • Fonts. Inter and Space Grotesk are loaded from inside the apps and this site (OFL license).

Permissions and why

Phone app

Camera
Only to read the pairing QR code. Nothing is recorded or sent. If you prefer, type the address and PIN by hand.
Internet, network state, multicast
To connect to the computer on the local network and find it with mDNS. Android requires the “Internet” permission for these connections; the app does not use it to send data to the internet.
Foreground service (connected device), wake lock, notifications
So the connection does not drop when the phone locks while the remote screen is open. A notification is shown while it runs; the notification permission (Android 13+) is for that.
Nearby devices (Bluetooth connect and advertise)
To make the phone a Bluetooth keyboard and mouse, to connect to the paired computer, to make the phone visible for pairing, and for the Bluetooth socket between phone and watch used by the watch’s laser. The app does not scan for devices around you. No location permission is asked.

Watch app

Vibrate
For time warnings.
Nearby devices (Bluetooth connect)
For the laser’s direct Bluetooth socket to the phone; if denied, the laser goes over the Data Layer, with some lag.
Foreground service, network state, wake lock, notifications
So the watch shows the remote again when the screen wakes rather than the watch face (Ongoing Activity), and for ambient mode.

Your control

  • When you uninstall the app, all its data on that device is deleted. The apps do not take part in Android’s cloud backup (allowBackup is off).
  • On the phone you can delete the presentation history in Settings; the recent computers and their PINs go when the app’s data is cleared (Android’s “Clear storage”) or the app is uninstalled. Removing a connected phone on the PC replaces the PIN.
  • You can turn off slide pictures, the update check and the start options in Settings.
  • You can withdraw permissions at any time in Android settings; the feature concerned (for example QR scanning) stops, and the app keeps working.

Children, changes, contact

Children. QuickRemote is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect personal data from anyone.

Changes. If this policy changes, the new version is published at this address with its date. The site’s source is on GitHub, where the history of changes can be seen.

Contact. For questions and requests, open an issue on GitHub.
